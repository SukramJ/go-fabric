## Differences from the brief

- **TLS kit defaults not registered.** RootNode, the only device type listing the TLS clusters, cannot be built by `device.New`, and chip serves the TLS servers on the root endpoint only; `node.Config.TLS` mounts them there (owner decision, above).
- **Not in the matter.js model at the pin:** UpdateMotionZoneOptions, the AmbientContext trigger, SFrameConfig (provider) and the CMAF CENC fields; their chip checks are left out.
- **Ids from 0:** chip's test mocks hand out ids from 1; the server follows chip's `IncrementingIdHelper` (CAID, CCDID, TLS endpoint from 0) and `GenerateConnectionID` (from 1); the ported tests assert the module's ids.
- **ClientCSR** publishes ProvisionedClientCertificates for the new entry; chip adds the entry without a change notification.
- **Reads** serve the full certificates (owner decision Q2); chip drops the payload without a large-payload transport (`TLSCertificateManagementCluster.cpp:136-166`).
- **ExpiryTime** is taken as the seconds until deallocation, as chip (`PushAVStreamTransportLogic.cpp:1216`), though the field is epoch-s (by_design candidate 5).
- **PushAV stream usage** checks SupportedStreamUsages (camera app `push-av-stream-manager.cpp:385-395`), though chip's log line names StreamUsagePriorities.
- **RemoveEndpoint** of an endpoint in use answers the model's EndpointInUse (owner decision Q3); chip's example delegate answers INVALID_IN_STATE (`:352`).
- **ProvideOffer** without StreamUsage for a new session is INVALID_COMMAND (chip's field is mandatory; the model makes it conditional); a re-offer without it takes the session's usage.

## Questions (answered by the owner on 2026-10-28, see above)

1. **TLS on endpoint 0.** The TLS servers are node-scoped, and chip serves them on the root endpoint only. Should we add a `node.Config` seam (for example `TLS *TLSConfig` with the CertificatePort and the stores), so the node mounts `tls.CertificateManagement` and `tls.ClientManagement` on endpoint 0 and hands their lookups to a camera's PushAvStreamTransport declaration? Or should mounting stay with a host-owned root extension?
2. **Certificate payload in reads.** Should ProvisionedRootCertificates and ProvisionedClientCertificates serve the full certificates (matter.js's generated behaviour)? Or should they drop the payload outside a large-payload transport, as chip does (`TLSCertificateManagementCluster.cpp:136-166`)? The second option needs a large-payload flag in the read context.
3. **RemoveEndpoint in use.** Should it answer INVALID_IN_STATE (chip's example delegate) or the model's cluster status EndpointInUse?

## by_design candidates

1. **BD-chip-WebRtcTransportProvider-RulesInServer.** matter.js `WebRtcTransportProviderServer.ts:14` at `862ad75f` is the generated behaviour. `camera.Provider` follows connectedhomeip `WebRTCTransportProviderCluster.cpp` at `6170af84` (validators `:133-209`, `:400-592`; session table `:295-398`; handlers `:595-1213`) behind the `ProviderPort`. It checks the streams against the camera's AvStream using the camera app's answers. Without these rules a controller could open a session on an unallocated stream or under privacy mode.
2. **BD-chip-PushAvStreamTransport-RulesInServer.** matter.js `PushAvStreamTransportServer.ts:14` is the generated behaviour. `camera.PushTransport` follows chip `PushAVStreamTransportLogic.cpp` (`:293-1603`, `:1903-2031`) behind the `PushTransportPort`, with the TLS endpoint, zone and stream checks against the node's servers. Without them a transport could push to an unvalidated URL or an unprovisioned TLS endpoint.
3. **BD-chip-TlsCertificateManagement-RulesInServer.** matter.js `TlsCertificateManagementServer.ts:14` is the generated behaviour. `tls.CertificateManagement` follows chip `TLSCertificateManagementCluster.cpp` (`:61-73`, `:230-575`) and its example delegate's tables (`CertificateTableImpl.cpp`, `IncrementingIdHelper.h`). The keys sit behind the `CertificatePort` and the tables in a settings record. Without a table, a TLS endpoint would have no certificate to name.
4. **BD-chip-TlsClientManagement-RulesInServer.** matter.js `TlsClientManagementServer.ts:14` is the generated behaviour. `tls.ClientManagement` follows chip `TLSClientManagementCluster.cpp` (`:53-275`) and its example delegate (`tls-client-management-instance.cpp:262-432`), and it is the certificate server's dependency checker. The push transport validates its TLSEndpointID against it.
5. **BD-chip-PushAv-ExpiryTimeAsSeconds.** chip's `ScheduleTransportDeallocate` takes ExpiryTime as the seconds until deallocation (`PushAVStreamTransportLogic.cpp:1216`, `:357-385`), although the field is an epoch-s value. We mirror it, since chip is the authority where matter.js has no logic.
