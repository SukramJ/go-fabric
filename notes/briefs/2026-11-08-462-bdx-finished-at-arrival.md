## The guarantee

**A protocol-state transition that needs no I/O is complete when the receive path returns for the datagram that caused it** — `route` (`client/exchange/manager.go:808-870`) on the controller, `dispatch` (`bridge/receive.go:97`) on the device — before the next datagram of that channel is decoded.

Implementation note (W9): the controller needed a change to hold this. Its UDP socket was served with `udp.Listener.Serve`, one goroutine per datagram, so two datagrams of one session could be handled in either order (reproduced: the device's ApplyUpdateRequest, sent after its BlockAckEOF, was handled first). It now uses `ServeOrdered` keyed by `message.SessionOrderKey`, the same key as the node.

Why this one: matter.js couples the OTA provider to the BDX flow exactly as we do (`OtaSoftwareUpdateProviderServer.ts:332-339` WaitForApply on `progressFinished`; `:432-437` force-close "when not known as completed") and has no explicit cross-exchange ordering (`ExchangeManager.ts:661`, one unawaited chain per datagram; `:283-401` no `await` before the exchange lookup; `:435` / `:489-492` the hand-over). Its effective guarantee is the runtime's: the earlier datagram's non-I/O continuation — queue read (`MessageExchange.ts:897`) → `finalizeTransfer` (`OutboundFlow.ts:115-117`) → `progressFinished` (`Flow.ts:139-141`) → WaitForApply — runs to the end before the next I/O callback (runtime property). chip gives the same by structure: run-to-completion dispatch (`ExchangeMgr.cpp:295-302`, `ExchangeContext.cpp:624`) with the BDX state step synchronous in the delegate (`TransferFacilitator.cpp:61`) and only the application output polled (`:86-96`; `TransferFacilitator.h:102-103`). Not adopted: per-session serialisation of handlers (the BDX flow waits for the peer's next Block on the same session — deadlock; and subscription reports would queue behind a timed invoke), and chip's decoupling (`OTAProviderExample.cpp:515-554` never reads the transfer — matter.js models the coupling, matter.js wins).
